# HAMLET Experience & First-Impression Standard
Version 1.0 — working design and content standard

## Product truth
HAMLET is a shortlet marketplace and lightweight operating platform. The immediate guest job is to find a suitable stay, understand the important facts and total cost, request it, pay when accepted, and manage the stay. The owner/manager job is to list and operate authorised properties with reliable booking and income records. The long-term ambition is the operating system for African shortlets; the launch footprint is local-first, with Lagos and Abuja as primary transactional markets and other locations only when supply and operations are ready.

## Experience promise
“Find it. Book it. Stay easy.” is the business-bible brand promise. “Shortlets, with accountability.” is the core USP. Consumer-facing copy should translate these into visible proof: accurate property details, honest availability, clear price and deposit breakdowns, explicit request/confirmation states, and a support route.

## First-impression rules
1. Explain what HAMLET does within five seconds: find and request a shortlet stay.
2. One primary action per view. The guest primary action is Find a stay / View stay / Request booking, depending on context.
3. Put search and available inventory before brand storytelling. The homepage flow is Brand → Intent → Search → Relevant stays → Trust proof → Help/partner paths.
4. Keep copy concise, specific and useful. Avoid repetitive city name drops, abstract slogans without proof, filler sections, and exaggerated claims.
5. Nigeria is the market ambition; Lagos is the current starting market. Never imply nationwide live inventory unless verified. Use “Across Nigeria, starting in Lagos” for brand positioning, and label actual city availability from live inventory.
6. No invented counts, reviews, verification, inspection, guarantees, amenities, availability, support hours, payment protection, or payout promises.
7. Never call a request a confirmed booking. Distinguish request submitted, partner checking, accepted/awaiting payment, paid/confirmed, cancelled and refunded.
8. Show nightly rate, number of nights, mandatory fees and refundable caution deposit separately before payment. Do not publish fee claims until commercial decisions are final.
9. Trust labels must be tied to the actual review evidence and status. A listing being visible is not itself proof of physical inspection or guarantee.
10. Use real property imagery with accurate captions. No generic stock that misrepresents an actual listing.

## Visual system
- Keep the approved V0 editorial architecture and recognisable layout; improve its craft rather than replacing the information architecture.
- Colour: deep forest/ink and warm ivory as structural anchors; cobalt, coral and citrus as deliberate high-energy accents. Use colour to signal hierarchy and action, not as random decoration. Footer, navigation, forms, cards and page backgrounds must share the same tokens.
- Typography: expressive serif for a few display moments; clear sans-serif for navigation, controls, data and body copy. No long paragraphs in oversized type.
- Photography: strong, well-cropped interiors and real property imagery; images should carry emotion and help users judge the stay.
- Motion: subtle scroll reveals, restrained stagger, responsive hover/press states and clear loading/empty/success/error feedback. No scroll hijacking, no essential content hidden until animation, and always respect reduced-motion.
- Responsive: design for 320, 360, 375, 390, 414px and desktop. Avoid oversized vertical sections, horizontal overflow, clipped controls, tiny tap targets and sticky elements covering content.

## Navigation and information architecture
- One global header/footer on every route, with a coherent public navigation and a clear Partner with us route.
- Mobile menu must open, close, navigate, show active context and be keyboard accessible.
- Workspace routes may add contextual workspace navigation, but must not strand users or replace the public navigation with unrelated legacy navigation.
- Remove duplicate page-level brand headers when the global header exists.
- Provide clear paths back to marketplace and account sign-in.

## Guest conversion checklist
- Search location, dates, guest count; filters for price, bedrooms, type and amenities where data supports them.
- Search results show meaningful photos, title, area/city, nightly rate, bedrooms, capacity and only evidenced trust indicators.
- Property detail answers: what is it, where is it (privacy-safe), who fits, what amenities are actually present, what does it cost, what are the rules, what dates can be requested, what happens next.
- Booking CTA states its real action (e.g. Request these dates), not vague “Book now” when approval is required.
- Preserve search choices when navigating back from a property.
- Empty states offer a useful next step: broaden dates/area, clear filters, or contact support.

## Quality assurance
For each route, inspect desktop and phone. Test every visible link, button, menu, selector and form. Test loading, empty, error and success states. Verify page metadata and copy against current product truth. Do not call the redesign complete until route-by-route visual QA and critical guest booking journey tests pass.
