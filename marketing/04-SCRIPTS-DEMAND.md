## Running demand content before the gate

Some of this material is worth producing in Phase 1 as **brand-building**, because the audience energy is live *now* and will not wait. When running pre-gate, apply these two changes to any script below:

1. **Swap the CTA.** Booking CTA → **waitlist** or **"see how we verify"** (`/safety`). Never send a viewer to `/stays` while it shows 0.
2. **Add an honest on-screen label** in the final 3 seconds: ***"Verified stays are being onboarded now. Join the list."*** This is consistent with the homepage's own empty state and protects the brand rather than damaging it.

Scripts marked **[PRE-GATE SAFE]** below can run in Phase 1 with those two changes. The others must wait.

### Why it works
It opens inside a joke every Nigerian already knows, which buys 16 seconds of voluntary attention that no brand film could purchase. "Ask your group chat" is a **share trigger** — it explicitly instructs the viewer to forward, which is the distribution model. The tonal cut at 22s is the mechanism: comedy earns the attention, the inspection footage converts it into belief. Ending on *"not flagged — rejected"* is deliberately harder than necessary, because the audience has been told "we take this seriously" by platforms that did not.

### Do not
- Do not use a real unverified property in a way that identifies it or its owner. Use a dressed recreation, labelled, or a HAMLET partner's consented property.
- Do not name or show a competitor's listing. Defamation risk in Nigeria is real and the point does not require it.
- Do not over-produce. If it looks like an advert, it dies.

### Pinned comment
> Inverter ≠ generator ≠ estate power. If you're working from the flat, filter for it. And if a host promised you something the listing doesn't say, report it from your booking record — that's what the issue log is for.

### Why it works
The blackout cold-open with audio only is a pattern interrupt that works *specifically because* the audience has lived it — the generator cutting out at 11pm is a shared national experience, and recognising it in two seconds produces the "this person gets it" response that drives follows. The script then does something rare: it converts a *feeling* into a **product specification** with three named, filterable values. "A caption is not information" is a line designed to be quoted back at other platforms. It also serves D2 (business travellers) without changing a word — a laptop at 9% is a lost client call, not just an annoyance.

### Do not
- Do not claim HAMLET guarantees power. It **discloses** the arrangement. The difference is legally and reputationally important: if a generator fails, the remedy is the issue-reporting route, not a broken promise from HAMLET.
- Do not mock hosts. The enemy is the vague caption, not the person who wrote it — S1-B needs those hosts as partners.
- Do not promise the filter exists for every listing until there are listings. Pre-gate, frame it as the standard.

### Why it works
Fear content usually performs, but fear content that only frightens people is worthless — it must **redirect the fear into a mechanism.** This script does that in a clean two-part structure: 20 seconds naming the exact scam pattern in the victim's own vocabulary, then 19 seconds showing a verifiable technical alternative. "A screenshot is not a booking" is the line to protect in the edit — it is short, quotable, and describes a real technical distinction (HMAC-SHA512 signed webhook vs. a JPEG) that no scammer can replicate. Pre-gate safe, because the safety story is true whether or not inventory exists.

### Do not

- Do not claim HAMLET is fraud-proof. Claim the *mechanism*. If a fraudulent partner ever gets through, an overclaim becomes the story.
- Do not publish in December. September–October is the window.

### Why it works
It takes the December 2025 outrage — a real, national, emotionally-charged news event — and redirects it into a practical reframe rather than just joining the complaint. The per-person number is the whole trick: ₦700,000 is a scandal, ₦50,000 is a plan. The **honest admission at 34s** ("we don't split the payment in the app yet") is counter-intuitively the strongest beat in the script — it proves the disclosure claim in the same breath as it makes a small confession, which is exactly the brand behaviour HAMLET is asking people to believe. It also pre-empts the comment that would otherwise appear.

### Do not
- **Do not imply split payment exists.** This is a hard product constraint; the honest beat is mandatory, not optional.
- Do not cite ₦700,000 as a HAMLET price or as a current market rate — it is a **December 2025 peak** from press reporting. Label the date on screen (as scripted).
- Do not promise the 3-bed at ₦300,000 is available. Pre-gate, present it as arithmetic, not inventory.
- Do not run the geo-fenced US/UK/CA ad version until inventory is live in the areas the ad targets.
