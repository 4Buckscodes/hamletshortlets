## Why the diaspora deserves its own pack

| Fact | Source | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **~495,000** diaspora air arrivals into Lagos, Dec 2024 — **90%** of ~550,000 total | MO Africa / NIDCOM / Tribune | The December demand is overwhelmingly diaspora, not tourism |
| **US = 27%** of international arrivals, then **UK, Canada, Italy, South Africa** | MO Africa via Monokromatik | Geo-targeting list. US first, by a wide margin |
| Shortlets earned **₦21bn / $13m** from **~6,000 bookings** in Dec 2024, avg **₦120,000/night** | MO Africa | Real, sized, recurring annual event |
| Nigeria's diaspora sends **≈$20–23bn/year** (~4× FDI) | Businessday | This audience has capital and is used to being sold to badly |
| Detty December projected to bring up to **$2bn FX by 2026** | MO Africa | Growing, not shrinking |
| Bookings went through **agents rather than platforms** — *"which speaks to trust concerns"* | MO Africa to Forbes Africa | The diaspora's default is a human they know. HAMLET must beat a *person*, not an app |
| Businessday warned Nigeria risks **"pricing itself out of its own billion-dollar cultural economy,"** pushing diaspora to **Accra, Cape Town, Zanzibar** | Businessday | The competitive threat is not another Lagos platform. It's Ghana |
| Diaspora **"shifting from sending money for consumption to structured investments"**; cautious **"after years of failed projects and inconsistent experiences"** | Baay Realty / Businessday | The dominant diaspora financial emotion is **caution**. Lead with accountability, never with excitement |

**Two things follow.**

1. **The diaspora books through people because platforms have not earned them.** So diaspora content must feel like advice from somebody who has done it, not advertising from a company. Register: peer, specific, slightly sceptical of itself.
2. **The booking window is September–November, not December.** Flights and flats are secured 2–4 months out. Anyone running diaspora acquisition in December is paying peak prices for people who have already booked. **The calendar in `06-EXECUTION-PLAN.md` is built around this.**

### Why it works
It opens in **Houston**, not Lagos — which immediately tells the diaspora viewer "this is made for me," a signal almost no Nigerian travel content bothers to send. The three-part structure (money → process → access) matches how a cautious buyer actually evaluates risk, and each part resolves with a *specific mechanism* rather than reassurance. The gate-code beat at 40s is the sleeper: it gives the viewer a **diagnostic they can use on any platform, including competitors'** — which is why it gets forwarded into family group chats. Advice that works even if you don't buy is the only advice that gets believed.

### Do not
- Do not use a Nigerian-only cast or setting. The opening frame must read as diaspora.
- Do not promise December availability. Pre-gate, route to `/how-it-works` or a waitlist — never to an empty `/stays`.
- Do not say "escrow." Server-verified settlement is a different mechanism.
- Do not run the paid geo-fenced version before inventory is live in the advertised areas. **An ad that converts into an empty marketplace is worse than no ad.**
- Do not launch in December.

### Why it works
This audience is making a **life decision**, and life-decision audiences reject enthusiasm. The script therefore refuses to sell Lagos — it assesses it, which is the only register that earns trust with people weighing a repatriation. Commercially it is the most valuable demand script in the pack: a 30-day stay at even a modest rate beats twelve weekend bookings, it costs almost nothing in turnover, and — critically — **it lands in the dead months.** Ikoyi falls from 85% December occupancy to 40% in January; VI from 80% to 35%. Long-stay relocation testers are the structural answer to that collapse, and no competitor is speaking to them.

### Do not
- Do not oversell Lagos or make it a homecoming-emotion piece. This audience has watched a decade of "come home" content and is immune.
- Do not promise visa, residency, or relocation advice.
- Do not claim monthly/long-stay discounts exist unless a host has actually configured one.
- Do not name specific ISPs or make speed claims.

### The 9-question checklist (chapter 6 — design this as a single screenshot-able frame)

> **BEFORE YOU PAY FOR ANY LAGOS SHORTLET, FROM ANYWHERE:**
>
> 1. Can you show me the **ownership document** — and does the name match the person listing it?
> 2. Has a **human being physically been inside**, and when?
> 3. Are the photos **of this flat**, taken during that visit?
> 4. What is the **power arrangement** — inverter, generator, or estate power? Who starts the generator, and until what time?
> 5. What is the **WiFi**, and does it hold at 2pm on a weekday?
> 6. What is the **total**, itemised — accommodation, fees, cleaning, deposit? Does it change at the door?
> 7. Is the **deposit** recorded separately, and what is the written inspection and refund process?
> 8. Am I paying **inside a platform that verifies the payment server-side** — or am I sending a transfer to a person?
> 9. If it is not as described, **what exactly happens**, and who is accountable?
>
> **If you cannot get a straight answer to #8, stop. That one is the whole thing.**

This is the **highest-ROI asset in the entire pack** and the only one that can ship immediately at full strength. It targets high-intent search queries with weak existing video answers; it has a multi-year shelf life; it is genuinely useful to people who never become customers, which is precisely why it gets trusted and forwarded; and it is the **only place with enough runtime to handle the 20% markup properly** — a 40-second reel cannot defend a fee, but nine minutes of itemised disclosure can.
